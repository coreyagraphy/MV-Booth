    Mix direction

    Loudness target: how loud the final export is (streaming platforms use −14). Vocal vs beat: + puts your voice further in front of the beat. Reverb: size of the room around the vocal. Delay: echo timed to the beat. Space amount: how much reverb and echo overall.

    Master (final polish)

    The last step on the whole song. Tone tilts the overall sound: warm = fuller low end, bright = more sparkle. Width makes the stereo image narrower or wider (100% = as mixed). Ceiling is the loudest peak allowed; −1 dB is safest for streaming.

    Two ways to tune. (1) Automix tune runs automatically every time you press Automix: fast and in the browser. (2) Studio tune (further down) is the high-quality version. It sends one take to the backend on your PC and gives you back a tuned take. Use Studio tune for the final vocal.

    Retune speed: how fast a note snaps to the right pitch. 0 ms = instant, the robotic "T-Pain" sound. 40–80 ms = natural. 200 ms+ = very gentle. Vibrato keep: 100% keeps your natural wobble, 0% irons it flat. Amount: how far toward the correct note (100% = all the way). Confidence: parts the engine isn't sure about are left alone; raise it to touch less. Preserve formants: keeps your voice sounding like you.

    Quick start

    1. Drag your beat onto the timeline (or Booth → Import beat).
    2. Click + Track → Lead, then click the round red button on the track to arm it.
    3. Click Enable mic in the Booth tab, then press R to record. A red RECORDING bar shows while it's going. Press R or Space to stop.
    4. Press Space to play it back; Space again pauses, Space again picks up where you paused.
    5. Press Automix. Press B to flip between your raw takes and the finished mix.
    6. Export ▾ → WAV or MP3.

    Edit tools (toolbar, keys 1–4)

    • ✥ Move (1 / V): drag a clip left or right. It snaps to the beat grid when Snap is on; hold Alt to place it freely.
    • ⇤ Trim (2): grab near the start or end of a clip and drag to shorten or lengthen it. Nothing is deleted; drag back to restore.
    • ✂ Cut (3): click inside a clip to cut it in two at that point. Use it to comp: cut, then pick a different take for each piece.
    • ◢ Fade (4): drag on the left half of a clip for a fade-in, on the right half for a fade-out.
    • Click a clip with any tool to select it. Delete removes it. The orange Clip box in the toolbar sets its volume and fades exactly.
    • Click an empty part of the timeline (or the ruler) to move the playhead there.

    Recording, loops and punch-ins

    • Count-in plays 1–2 bars of click before recording starts. Click (C) turns the metronome on.
    • Loop record: drag across the ruler to pick a section, press L, press R. Every time it loops you get a new take.
    • Punch-in: Shift-drag on the ruler over the part you want to redo (it turns red), press P, press R. It plays a bar or two before, records only that part, plays a bar after, and stops on its own. The fix blends in smoothly.
    • Takes: the 3 ▾ chip on a track opens its take lanes. Use puts a whole take on the track; Comp swaps that take into the selected clip; ★ marks your favourite.

    Mixer

    Each column is one track. Fader: volume (up = louder, double-click = back to 0 dB). Pan: left or right in the speakers. Reverb: how much room sound on that track. Echo: how much tempo-synced delay. M mute, S solo. The Master column turns everything up or down while you listen.

    When you press Automix, the engine balances levels itself; your faders then nudge its choices up or down from there.

    Presets

    • Rap · Upfront: vocal sits on top of the beat, short plate reverb, dotted-1/8 echo. Hard-hitting and clear.
    • Rap · Spacey: big hall reverb and quarter-note echo for melodic or atmospheric tracks.
    • Rap · Club loud: mastered loud (−9 LUFS) with a tight room. For club and DJ play.
    • VO · Dry & clear: voice-over: almost no reverb, no echo, broadcast-friendly level.
    • Podcast: dry, even, −16 LUFS (the podcast standard).
    • Tuning presets (Tune tab): Natural fixes notes and keeps your character; Hard-tune gives the robotic effect.

    Tuning

    Automix tune happens automatically when you press Automix. Choose Off / Natural / Tight / Hard. The key is detected for you, or set it yourself.

    Studio tune is the best-quality version. It sends one take to the tuning program on your PC (the backend) and adds a new tuned take; your original stays safe in the take lane. It finds your pitch with an AI pitch tracker (CREPE), snaps each note toward the key, and rebuilds the sound with a vocoder (WORLD) that keeps your voice's tone. Anything it isn't sure about is left exactly as you sang it. The report lists every phrase: teal = tuned, red = left alone.

    Retune speed 0 = robot, 40–80 ms = natural. Vibrato keep 100% = keep your wobble. Amount 100% = all the way to the note.

    Automix (bottom bar)

    Eight steps run in order: Tune (pitch) → Align (lines doubles up with the lead) → Double (AI stereo doubles if you turned on 2X) → Clean (noise, breaths, pops, harsh S's) → Tone (EQ) → Dynamics (evens out loud and quiet words) → Space (reverb, echo, beat balance) → Master (final loudness). Click any step to switch it off. The Engine tab explains every decision it made.
